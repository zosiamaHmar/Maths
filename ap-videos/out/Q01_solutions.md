# Question 1 — Sum of an AP

## (i) Find the sum of the AP 8, 3, −2, … to 22 terms.

- **Find the common difference: second term minus first term**
  - `d = 3 − 8 = −5`
- **Write down what we know**
  - `a = 8,   d = −5,   n = 22`
- **Use the sum formula**
  - `Sₙ = {n|2} [2a + (n − 1)d]`
- **Put the numbers in**
  - `S₂₂ = {22|2} [2(8) + (22 − 1)(−5)]`
- **First do the brackets and the multiplications inside**
  - `= 11 [16 + (21)(−5)]`
  - `= 11 [16 + (−105)]`
- **Now add inside the big bracket**
  - `= 11 × (−89)`
- **Finally multiply**
  - `= −979`

**Answer:** `S₂₂ = −979`

- *Check — Quick check: the terms are going down by 5 each time, so the sum is negative*: `a₂₂ = 8 + 21(−5) = −97`; `S₂₂ = 11 × (8 − 97) = 11 × (−89) = −979`

## (ii) Find the sum of the AP 2, 7, 12, … to 10 terms.

- **Find the common difference**
  - `d = 7 − 2 = 5`
- **Write down what we know**
  - `a = 2,   d = 5,   n = 10`
- **Use the sum formula**
  - `Sₙ = {n|2} [2a + (n − 1)d]`
- **Put the numbers in**
  - `S₁₀ = {10|2} [2(2) + (10 − 1)(5)]`
- **First do the brackets and the multiplications inside**
  - `= 5 [4 + (9)(5)]`
  - `= 5 [4 + (45)]`
- **Now add inside the big bracket**
  - `= 5 × 49`
- **Finally multiply**
  - `= 245`

**Answer:** `S₁₀ = 245`

- *Check — Quick check with the last term*: `a₁₀ = 2 + 9(5) = 47`; `S₁₀ = 5 × (2 + 47) = 5 × 49 = 245`

## (iii) Find the sum of the AP −37, −33, −29, … to 12 terms.

- **Find the common difference**
  - `d = −33 − (−37) = −33 + 37 = 4`
- **Write down what we know**
  - `a = −37,   d = 4,   n = 12`
- **Use the sum formula**
  - `Sₙ = {n|2} [2a + (n − 1)d]`
- **Put the numbers in**
  - `S₁₂ = {12|2} [2(−37) + (12 − 1)(4)]`
- **First do the brackets and the multiplications inside**
  - `= 6 [−74 + (11)(4)]`
  - `= 6 [−74 + (44)]`
- **Now add inside the big bracket**
  - `= 6 × (−30)`
- **Finally multiply**
  - `= −180`

**Answer:** `S₁₂ = −180`

- *Check — Quick check with the last term*: `a₁₂ = −37 + 11(4) = 7`; `S₁₂ = 6 × (−37 + 7) = 6 × (−30) = −180`

## (iv) Find the sum of the AP 1/15, 1/12, 1/10, … to 11 terms.

- **Find the common difference. We need a common denominator, the LCM of 12 and 15, which is 60**
  - `d = {1|12} − {1|15}`
  - `= {5|60} − {4|60} = {1|60}`
- **Write down what we know**
  - `a = {1|15},   d = {1|60},   n = 11`
- **Use the sum formula**
  - `Sₙ = {n|2} [2a + (n − 1)d]`
- **Put the numbers in**
  - `S₁₁ = {11|2} [2({1|15}) + (11 − 1)({1|60})]`
- **Multiply inside the bracket**
  - `= {11|2} [{2|15} + 10 × {1|60}]`
  - `= {11|2} [{2|15} + {10|60}]`
  - `= {11|2} [{2|15} + {1|6}]`
- **Add the fractions. The LCM of 15 and 6 is 30**
  - `= {11|2} [{4|30} + {5|30}]`
  - `= {11|2} × {9|30}`
  - `= {11|2} × {3|10}`
- **Multiply the tops together and the bottoms together**
  - `= {33|20}`

**Answer:** `S₁₁ = {33|20}  (= 1.65)`

- *Check — Quick check as a decimal*: `33 ÷ 20 = 1.65`

## (v) Find the sum of the AP 0.6, 1.7, 2.8, … to 100 terms.

- **Find the common difference**
  - `d = 1.7 − 0.6 = 1.1`
- **Write down what we know**
  - `a = 0.6,   d = 1.1,   n = 100`
- **Use the sum formula**
  - `Sₙ = {n|2} [2a + (n − 1)d]`
- **Put the numbers in**
  - `S₁₀₀ = {100|2} [2(0.6) + (100 − 1)(1.1)]`
- **First do the brackets and the multiplications inside**
  - `= 50 [1.2 + (99)(1.1)]`
  - `= 50 [1.2 + (108.9)]`
- **Now add inside the big bracket**
  - `= 50 × 110.1`
- **Finally multiply**
  - `= 5505`

**Answer:** `S₁₀₀ = 5505`

- *Check — Quick check with the last term*: `a₁₀₀ = 0.6 + 99(1.1) = 109.5`; `S₁₀₀ = 50 × (0.6 + 109.5) = 50 × 110.1 = 5505`
